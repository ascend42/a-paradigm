# Gnomon console: design

The tokens, components, zones and rules are in `design-system.yaml` next to this file. The rendered system is `specimen.html` (every component in every state, day, night and day without motion, drawn with the real `packages/gnomon-gui/static/styles.css`). The spec it serves is `docs/specs/gnomon.md` v3.4, and the colour values are the G-GUI-10 fixture verbatim. The direction was set in session `2026-10-15-gnomon-aesthetic`, the voice in `2026-10-21-gnomon-voice`, and the system was codified in `2026-10-21-design-systems`.

## The read

An instrument for one operator running an armada. You glance at it many times a day and open it a few times a day to decide. It is exact, calm and quietly beautiful: a made object that reads a larger order.

Gnomon is a desktop instrument. Phone layouts are out of scope for now; a true mobile version will come later as its own work, so nothing here is bent to fit a narrow screen.

## Two modes of one object

- **Day is papercraft:** cut-paper sheets on a grained ground, ink lines, folded paper caps, and pressable ink keys with a hard offset shadow. This deliberately overrides the `default-warm-paper` tell, because the founder wants the papercraft feel.
- **Night is phosphor:** a near-black ground, glowing outlines, bracketed keys and glyph caps. Only the wordmark, the caps, the chosen segment and the tall-view key glow.

## Principles

These settle trade-offs when a new piece of UI doesn't fit what is already here.

1. **Colour means one thing.** The six mineral hues mean *which project* and nothing else. Three semantic colours mean three things: vermilion is failed, amber is a warning (held countdowns, GATE), violet is KNOT. Everything else, status included, is ink. A live mic, a connected kernel, an "ok" and the APPROVED stamp read by their mark (a filled or hollow dot, ✓, a word) and their weight, never by a hue.
2. **The edge says whether it presses.** Anything you can click is a key: one height for every key (`--key-h`, 28px), with a dark key edge. A quiet key changes the look (no offset, softer ink), never the size. A chip is a status label nobody clicks: an 18px line, a faint rule edge. Fields sit between them, with an edge in the secondary text colour (5.8:1 on the page, 6.5:1 on the sheet) that turns to ink on hover and focus. Eight small bordered styles have become three looks: the chip, the small key and the reply's labelled part.
3. **Four sizes, then weight.** Type has four steps: 10.5 for labels, 11.5 for controls, 13.5 for reading and 17 for titles. Past that, hierarchy is weight, case and face: caps for labels, mono uppercase titles at night, Fraunces for titles by day. If something seems to need a fifth size, it needs a different weight.
4. **A 2px grid.** Every padding, margin, gap and inset is an even number of pixels. The only exceptions are a few fixed layout dimensions (the 76px Workshop column, the 140px pile tail).
5. **Say what to do.** A label tells you the next move in the product's own words. A card that voice can decide quotes the exact phrase the voice grammar accepts for it, such as `say “claude admin approve card 3” · reversible`, or says `hand only`. A refusal says what didn't happen and why in plain words, then `next:`. In the GUI the wire code, the symbol and the rule id (`FORBIDDEN`, `#gnomon-voice`, `G-VOX-06`) stay out of the line; they stay in the raw view, the kernel log and the CLI. The founder ruled this on 2026-10-23, after objecting to raw codes several times; it replaces "a refusal gives its code". Voice send refusals already follow it, and the other GUI refusals are migration batch `refusal-copy`.
6. **The kernel's regions can't be hidden.** The voice pill, the link status, run mode, the alerts band, handle words, the held countdown and card bodies stay visible whatever preset, pane or mod is in front.
7. **Model text never looks like a key.** Anything built from the agent's words has a dashed edge and a second cue, and is never drawn as a decision key.

## Type: the mono spine

JetBrains Mono sets everything in both modes. Fraunces appears only on the wordmark and titles by day. Long text gets line-height 1.6 and lines of at most 80 characters. The four-step scale costs two places something, and both are handled on purpose. The Workshop's 76px column grows from 9 to 10.5, so a cap's question truncates at about nine characters and keeps its full text in the tooltip and accessible name. The first-run key digest drops from 34 to 17, and it holds up for comparison by weight, spacing and groups of four rather than by size.

## Motion: the lively workshop

Things move only when something happens, and they move quietly. A decision sheet fades in quickly, with no flutter. Deciding stamps it APPROVED, landing straight, then it fades out and the pile closes up over it; no tilt or spin on the way out (the founder, 2026-10-23: "get rid of the whole card tilt and spin aspect and can go with a simple and tasteful fade out"). A failing session droops once and turns its edge vermilion; it never shakes. Caps bob only while their session streams. A voice approval shows its 3-second countdown before the stamp. Reduced motion makes all of it instant.

## The zones

- **Header.** Four things: the Gnomon ▾ wordmark menu, the ⌘K palette field, the quiet kernel status (a link dot, the browser-risk chip) and the voice pill. Every control is 28px and the row never wraps. Rare tools (layout, theme, mods, settings) live in the wordmark menu and the palette, never as keys in the row.
- **Alerts band.** The strip under the header carries kernel alerts only: a re-pinned kernel key and a voice dev build. They stay until you acknowledge them or the cause goes away.
- **Toasts.** Everything else that tells you something happened is one toast style, at the chat column's top-right edge, over the desk so it never pushes the work down. That covers saved, not sent, refusals, voice turning itself off, an update that's ready and standing-grant offers. Outcomes leave after six seconds; failures stay until you close them. Toasts no longer appear bottom-right or bottom-centre, and there is no loose update banner.
- **Sessions (left).** + New session, search, active sessions grouped by project with a swatch and a state note, past sessions collapsed, and a bounded mod slot at the bottom.
- **Centre.** The chat header (title, project, model, run mode, more) with its small controls as 24px keys, then the chat with timestamps, durations and a collapsible activity fold. Offer slips sit under their reply, this session's pending cards are pinned above the composer, and there is one composer with one send key.
- **Stack (right).** The global stack as a paper pile: decision cards first, most urgent first, then alerts, then the slip of loops waiting for trust at the foot. Each card and alert names what it relates to by glyph and word (↻ loop, ◇ session). The cards fan on hover and on keyboard focus so a buried key can always be reached. Each card shows in the stack and pinned in its session, and deciding it in either place removes both.
- **Workshop.** The caps mod, a footer strip by default or a 76px column on either edge: Needs you, Working, and one quiet status line.
- **Overlays.** The palette, new session, Settings, Mods and the Voice settings drawer, where teaching happens inline under its row. Toasts step aside while an overlay is open.

## Voice

Voice is one choice, not two. A trust ladder orders the presets by how much voice does on its own (Off, Talk, Dictate, Send word, Pieces), and the mic follows the rung. Each rung says what it does in a line, and what it costs when it costs something. The header pill always names the rung plus one live fact, with a filled ink dot when the mic is open. When the voice app turns the mic off by itself, the dot goes hollow and the fact gives a short cause (`mic stopped: input kept changing`), with the whole sentence and Restart in the ladder. The input device is never on the pill's face. It shows in the pill's title and in Voice settings › input, the sheet's first row, where you pick the system default or a named mic and watch its level. It shows a vermilion reason when speech wasn't sent. A voice-sent turn runs under the run mode and Always rules like a typed one, so nothing on the pill marks it (TD-2026-10-23-939). The pill opens only the ladder; everything configured lives in the Voice settings sheet. Nobody is ever asked to say the product name.

## CLI voice: instrument readout

Aligned lowercase labels, numbers first, items joined by ` · `, and ✓ ✕ ◆ marks. There is no emoji. Every refusal ends with a `next:` line.

## Loops (session 2026-10-22-gnomon-loops-ui)

Loops are background work you trust, then mostly ignore. A loop shows itself in two places: the header's kernel status ("loops 1 · 2 waiting") and the stack. Anything that still needs you goes to the stack. A loop-choice is a card. A failure, stop or kernel pause is an alert, a flat sheet that decides nothing. Loops waiting for trust sit in a dashed slip at the foot. A loop that simply finished says so in a 6-second toast. The Loops view is a leaf over the centre column: a list grouped by project with a pinned needs-you group, and a detail with the node graph, keys that name their cost, and inline budgets. Trusting a loop unfolds its card into the centre, so you read the whole definition before you press Trust.

This session changed two earlier rules. The stack now holds alerts as well as decision cards, which was the founder's call over Mika's objection. Toasts are recorded at the chat column's edge, which is where they already ship. The direction is in [DIRECTION.md](../../sessions/2026-10-22-gnomon-loops-ui/DIRECTION.md) and [direction.html](../../sessions/2026-10-22-gnomon-loops-ui/refs/direction.html). The components, rules and build requirements are in `design-system.yaml`. The graph's fit for three of the four spec loops is still a build requirement, and Today's ruler is a future list view.

## Local model (session 2026-10-22-gnomon-local-model)

Setting up a local model is a section of the Settings sheet, "Local model · kernel-owned", between Voice and Permissions. Its form opens inline under its row (the shipped Mods Configure form, now the `settings-form` component): server, address, model, who may use it, and an Advanced fold for the endpoint name and the runner. The key names its cost, **Save · root card**. Because a typed password is the expensive step, nothing that can be checked without signing waits for the card: the page checks the address and model, then the kernel checks the server, read-only, before the card is raised (rule no-password-for-a-typo). While a sheet is open, toasts are hidden and the stack sits under the scrim, so the row says "card waiting" in place and offers **Decide in the stack**, which focuses the card, never its Allow (rule form-says-where-the-card-is). The card is a grant with the new **⊙ gnomon** family and lists every field it signs. Turning the model off takes no card; the GUI asks once, inline, only when loops would stop. A loop that can't start for want of a local model says so in its detail, pins to needs you, and, when a scheduled start is refused, raises a `refused-start` alert, so it never fails silently. The reference is [local-model.html](../../sessions/2026-10-22-gnomon-local-model/refs/local-model.html). Its CSS lives in `styles.css` with the rest of the GUI.

## Folders (session 2026-10-23-gnomon-folder-picker)

You choose a folder; you don't type one (rule paths-are-chosen). New session lists up to six recent folders, each with its project swatch, the repo name, the path shortened with ~ and when it was last used. The centre session's folder is already chosen (else the most recent), so ⌘N then Enter opens a session there. **Choose folder…** asks the kernel to open the Mac's own chooser; the kernel draws it, so it works in the wrapper and in a browser on the same Mac, and the wrapper keeps no page IPC. Typing a path is folded under **Type a path ▸**, and shown open only when the kernel can't open a chooser. The kernel checks every choice before Open is enabled. A file, a missing path or a folder macOS won't let Gnomon read gets a ✕ line with the next step. The home folder is allowed, with an amber ◆ warning that auto-edits is off there. The sheet stays open until the session opens, so a refusal shows in place. The same `folder-field` (compact, without recents) replaces the typed paths in Settings › Local model runner and Mods › Add from folder. Dragging a folder in from Finder waits until after 8.0; the Mac's chooser already accepts one. GUI copy says "folder", not "cwd".

The same session made one key height the rule: `--key-h`, 28px, for every key in every zone. Quiet keys, fold keys, segment keys and the chat header's glyph keys were 24px beside 28px keys, which the founder saw as inconsistent button heights. The reference is [folder-picker.html](../../sessions/2026-10-23-gnomon-folder-picker/refs/folder-picker.html), with every key before and after on board K. Both changes are pending migration batches (`folder-field`, `key-height`).

## Dimensions and the board (proposed, session 2026-11-23-gnomon-board-medium)

Proposed for milestone S1.1 while the founder was away. Nothing in this section is decided; the entries are `proposed` in `design-system.yaml` (the slip states are `contested`, as the offer slip is), and the rendered proposal is [specimen-board.html](specimen-board.html), day and night. Mika reviewed it rendered and Aegis ruled on it the same day (`.paradigm/research/gnomon-canvas/aegis-s1.1-design-rulings.md`); what they changed is proposed too, and is marked `mika:` and `aegis:` in the YAML and "added in review" in the specimen.

A **dimension** is the space things are shown in: it has a title, belongs to a project and has sessions attached. A **board** is the first medium, a whiteboard you and your agents draw on together. You open a board; the board shows a dimension. Where both words appear, the dimension comes first: "auth handoff › board". The drawing engine is Excalidraw, and none of its own look ships: no hand-drawn font, no wobble, none of its toolbar, and not its selection violet, which sits 0.057 from KNOT's.

The board is a leaf over the centre column, as Loops is, with one difference: it stops above the session's pinned cards and the composer, so you keep talking while it is open. Its head names the dimension and the medium; the line under it says which project, which sessions are attached and how it was opened; its foot lists the hands that have drawn. The stack, the header and the composer are never under a board, and nothing inside a board is ever a key. While a card is focused the board gives up the keyboard and says so: "A card has the keys · click the board to draw". The first press only wakes the board; it draws nothing. At a 1024 window the centre is 424px wide, so the head drops "› board" and then truncates the title, and nothing in the leaf wraps. The leaf also has a loading state and a failed state with Try again in its head.

The tools are a rail down the leaf's left edge, a strip of the sheet graduated like a scale rule, with the system's own segment keys carrying glyphs drawn for Gnomon. The letters sit in the rule's margin. No tool uses A or D. The percent is a key like the others. The rail needs 472px; when pinned cards leave it less, zoom folds into the percent key and tools fold into a ⋯ key from the bottom of the list, so the rail never scrolls.

Content has its own colours, `content-palette:v1`, kept apart from the console's. Six hues (lemon, leaf, sky, cobalt, orchid, raspberry) sit in the gaps the instrument leaves, with ink, graphite and paper bound to the theme. A custom colour is allowed, with a fixed night transform. Every content colour stays at least 0.06 in OKLab from every mineral and semantic colour; the two closest reserved colours are themselves 0.045 apart. A custom colour that lands too close is not used and the tray offers the nearest swatch; the renderer draws any stored colour that no longer passes as ink or as no fill. A fill may not come close to ink or the key edge either, so nothing drawn can wear the primary key's colours. This carries out the founder's decision for free hues (TD-2026-11-24-500), made over my recommendation of paper stocks only.

Colour never says who drew a mark. Your marks are solid; an agent's are dashed, and nobody can choose the dash. Each agent has a seal worked out from its id: a cut, a bead on the rim and a letter. Solid means the host vouches and dashed means it does not. There are three hands: you (solid, a plain pin), an agent the host named (dashed, a seal), and the unknown hand, a mark whose row names no one the host vouches for (dashed, no seal, a hollow pin). Nothing unvouched is ever solid. A sub-agent that named itself will have a seal with a dashed rim and the word "claimed", always attached to its parent's seal; that waits for S1.4, because the host cannot carry a self-claimed name yet. An agent's text always has its dashed box and seal, and under 50% zoom, where dashes close up, agent marks carry a small ring.

The device is the pin. Paper on the board (a sticky, or the stub for a node this build can't draw) is pinned to the desk and casts a short hard shadow. The stub leads with the word "kind", so a kind named like an instruction reads as a name. On a live node that shadow will also tell the time: it lengthens in five steps as the reading ages, always beside a printed "as of". S1 has no live nodes, so S1 ships the pin and the shadow at rest; the lengthening waits for the live tray. That use of shadow as a signal needs an override of the recorded rule that shadow is material only, and the override is proposed, not in force.

`⌥B` belongs to the desk. It reopens or folds a board the session already has and never accepts a live offer from elsewhere on the desk, so an offer's Open shows no key.

What the founder should look at first: the two words and where they appear; the six hues with no red, orange or violet, beside the facts that a custom orange passes the distance rule and that the sky and cobalt papers are nearly one colour; the unknown hand; the ⌥B key; the short rail; and the shadow override. Refuse-or-snap is settled by Aegis as refuse at the picker, resolve at the renderer.

## An agent's hand on the board (proposed, pass S1.4 P4-design, 2026-11-25)

Proposed while the founder was away, for the milestone in which agents draw. The build goes ahead on it and the founder may overrule any part. Mika reviewed it rendered and Aegis ruled on three items the same day; what they changed is in `design-system.yaml` under `luna_final_s14` and `aegis`. The entries are component `draw-grant`, `author-seal.strokes`, `author-seal.seal.ring`, `author-seal.naming`, `author-seal.session_hint`, `board-leaf.hands_foot`, `board-leaf.said`, `board-leaf.turn_strip.luna_s14`, `content_palette.contrast_rule`, and rules `small-hand-is-a-ring` and `draw-key-is-not-a-decision`. The drawing is [specimen-board.html, section 9](specimen-board.html#agent-hand). The turn strip under the board, ruled on 2026-11-24, is in `board-leaf.turn_strip`.

An agent's stroke is to be dashed along its whole length, so it no longer needs a seal on it all the time; the seal comes on hover and selection, as it does for a shape. An agent's frame is to have one edge, dashed, with its seal always on the top edge because the frame's title is the agent's words. Both rest on engine facts that P4a has yet to show. Until it does, a stroke and a frame keep the always-on seal they have today. Text keeps its dashed box and seal. An arrowhead stays solid, because the engine never dashes one. Colour still says nothing about who drew a mark.

The dash is ruled as the engine can keep it: one pattern in board units, round caps, seen as about three widths on and two off. It shrinks with the board. A mark too short to show two dashes carries a small ring at every zoom.

Seals do not shrink with the board, so under 50% zoom each one becomes a 6px ring with no letter and no name, and every mark that is not yours carries one. Hovering a mark gives back the full seal with the hand's name beside it, and selecting one mark also puts the whole line (name, time, session) under the leaf's head. An agent's mark must never read as an empty box, so when a text's own letters are under 4px tall on the screen, the box round it gives way to a dashed rule under it. At night your own pin fills under 50%, so solid is yours and hollow is not. No mark can take keyboard focus in S1.4, so a keyboard user reads the hands from the foot.

The foot of the leaf lists every attached session's agent, with a quiet key beside each that reads **may draw** or **may not draw**. One press changes it, and stopping an agent is always one press. The key shows only what the board has confirmed, sends the state it means and never a toggle, and drops a press it cannot act on. It is a setting, drawn so that nobody takes it for a decision: no ink fill, no cost, none of the words Allow, Deny or Trust, no letter or voice phrase, and it is never raised, moved or focused by anything an agent does. Counts sit in fixed slots and hands stand in the order their sessions attached. When the foot is too narrow, the hands that do not fit go behind a small key that opens them as a menu upward over the board.

Every Claude Code session's agent is called Claude. When two of them are on one board, each hand adds its session's name after ◇ where there is room, and a sentence says "Claude in fix-auth".

The words: when an agent's turn ends the leaf says once, to a screen reader, "Claude added 3 marks"; sighted, the same news is the marks, the foot's count and the turn strip's tail, "drew 5 on the board". When an agent removes a mark you were dragging the leaf says "Claude removed the mark you were moving". In the turn strip a message on its way reads "sending"; "waiting" is kept for a message queued behind a running turn. A turn that ended in an error says so first, straight after the name, because at the row's end it read quieter than the reply at night and a heavier weight did not help.

Custom colours gain a legibility rule beside the distance rule. A line needs 3:1 against both the desk and paper, in both themes, and 4.5:1 when it colours a text mark. A neutral line is not exempt. A fill must leave ink on it at 4.5:1. A mark's line needs 3:1 against its own fill, checked when the mark is written, for every hand. The tray refuses in plain words ("too faint to see on the board") and offers a swatch that works.

What the founder should look at first: the draw key and its two phrases; one press to let an agent draw, with no confirm; the dashed rule that replaces the box round agent text too small to read; "sending" in place of "waiting"; an error leading its row; and the custom colours that now pass as a fill and not as a line (orange, gold, a bright blue).

## The short rail, the foot and the Dimensions list (proposed, pass design-foot, 2026-11-25)

Proposed while the founder was away, on Mika's rendered review of the first board build that runs in the app. The build goes ahead on it and the founder may overrule any part. The entries are `board-leaf.heights`, `heights_measured`, `too_short`, `check`, `keep_words`, `widths_narrow`, `hands_foot.gives_way` and `hands_foot.narrow`; `board-rail.short_windows`, `fold` and `menus_scroll`; the new component `dimension-list`; and rules `list-rows-are-not-cards` and `quiet-lines-are-lower-case`. The drawing is [specimen-board.html, section 10](specimen-board.html#foot-pass). Mika reviewed it rendered and Aegis ruled on it the same day; every change they asked for is in the record under `luna_final` and `aegis`, and section 10 was redrawn. The redraw has not been reviewed.

The record's heights were wrong, and are now the ones Mika measured in the app. A 1024 × 768 window with no pinned cards gives the board 419px, not 538, so the whole rail (472px) is never drawn there. With pinned cards the board has 224 to 248px, and at a 640-high window 156px or less. A board under 230px is ordinary, so the rail keeps folding: Text goes into ⋯, then Sticky, and last the group ticks and the spacer. That last, tight rail keeps its graduated edge, because the edge is the rail and a bare strip is how a rail looks when it takes no keys. The tool in hand is always on the rail and takes the last slot; it brings no tick with it, so choosing a tool swaps one key and never two.

Under 140px there is no room for any rail. The leaf used to show a head, one line and blank ground. It now shows the board, held still as it is when the link is down: you can look, pan and zoom, and the line says "No room to draw · decide a card or make the window taller". With no card pinned it says only "make the window taller". The foot stays and the draw keys work: too short is its own state, not the link being down, so stopping an agent is still one press. Words being typed when the room goes are kept on the page and said, never dropped.

The foot's check no longer shows a number. The number was the log's row, not the board's, so a new empty board read "you 0" beside "Checked up to change 79". It now names its point in words: "checked up to the last change", "checked up to 2 changes ago", "checked up to an older change", "nothing to check yet", "not checked yet", and in the failed colour "doesn't match the saved board". When the hands and their draw keys need the foot, the check gives way first and whole; it is never cut and has no short form. The hands alone decide whether it is in the foot, so it never comes and goes with its own words. It is always on the open board's row of the Dimensions list, which the keyboard reaches at every width; Mika first recommended the hands menu and withdrew it, because that menu does not exist with one agent at a 1024 window.

When the check is not in the foot the leaf still says when something is wrong. A mismatch is said in the line under the head with its fact, "This board is out of date", in the failed colour, which no passing line ever wears; that line outranks every other. A board that is open and not yet checked leads the same line with "not checked yet". A passing check that is not in the foot is pointed to by nothing: its absence claims nothing.

The line under the head has one rule for case and colour. A quiet readout is lower case in the secondary colour: the usual line, "opening the board…", "folding the board…", "saving…". A line that takes its place to say something is a sentence in ink, with the fact before the dot and the next step after it. Every line uses contractions. "Could not be held" is gone; the two lines about missing marks say that the saved board still has them. They do not yet say what to do next, because nothing the page does today brings those marks back, and the builder has that question.

"Dimensions ▾" now opens what its ▾ promises: the leaf's own menu, hung under the key, inside the leaf. Each row has a mark (a filled dot for a dimension that is open somewhere, a hollow one for a folded one), the title, the time of its last change, and a second line that says who is attached and what a press will do: "folded · opening starts it again", "◇ billing-ui · opening attaches this session". The board open here is always the first row, above the rows that scroll, marked by a rule, and carries the check. The rows keep their order while the list is open, so nothing an agent draws can move a row under the pointer. When a short leaf leaves no room for the list, the key opens the palette's list instead. Rows are set apart by spacing; a row is never a box, a card or a key. `/boards` and ⌘K still open the palette's find list with the same rows, because that has to work when no board is open and a project can hold hundreds of dimensions.

The leaf is supported down to 300px, which is what the Decide preset gives at a 1024 window. There the head gives up its ornament before a control: "› board", then the ◰ glyph, then the esc key (the Esc key, "show chat" and ⌥B still fold the board; esc stays whenever "show chat" would not fold it), and the title is cut last. In the foot the first hand's name gives way and its seal, count and draw key stay.

What the founder should look at first: the check without a number and where it lives; the two signs under the head (the failed colour for a mismatch, the "not checked yet" lead); the board shown still in a window too short to draw in; the chosen tool taking Select's place on the shortest rail; the esc key leaving the head at 303px; and the Dimensions list's rows and their second lines.

## Any hand may change any mark (proposed, pass E-design, 2026-11-26)

The founder decided that any hand, you, any agent and later another person, may change or remove any mark on a board; that every change is attributed and easy to undo, with undo symmetric between hands; and that several agents may work on one board while a person watches. The model is his. The look and the words here are proposed while he is away, and the build goes ahead on them. The entries are `author-seal.other_hands`, `author-seal.restored`, the new component `undo-key`, `board-leaf.turn_strip.notice`, `board-leaf.said.undo`, `board-leaf.said.changed_since`, `board-leaf.supervision` and `draw-grant.edit_anything`, with rules `another-hand-is-signed` and `undo-key-is-not-a-decision`. The drawing is [specimen-board.html, section 11](specimen-board.html#edit-anything). Mika reviewed it rendered ("buildable with changes") and Aegis read it the same day; the final pass took their changes, recorded under `luna final 2026-11-26` and `aegis` keys, and added the component `board-trace`.

A mark keeps the look of the hand that drew it, whoever changes it later. If Claude restyles your box, it stays solid and carries no seal. The hand that changed it signs at the mark's foot, where a signature goes on paper: a small ring for an agent and a small filled disc for you. It sits just under the bottom edge, inset from the corner, because the engine's resize handle owns the corner itself; on a line it sits near the end of the last run, clear of any label. These are the ring and the pin the board already uses, so solid still means yours and hollow still means not yours. Hover gives back the full seal and a chip that says "changed by Claude". If another hand writes words into your mark, those words never read as yours. In a sticky or a label they get that hand's dashed rule along the bottom of the label, with its seal at the right end, always on. In a text mark they get the whole dashed box, as an agent's text does. Words read as yours only when you wrote all of them. These signs keep their form under 50% zoom, and they never land on another hand's ring.

A mark put back by an undo wears the look of the hand that first drew it. Claude's text that you put back stays dashed and sealed as Claude's, and your disc at its foot says you put it back. Your sticky put back by Claude keeps your pin and never Claude's seal. Its edge is dashed, though, because Aegis's rule makes a mark solid only when both hands are yours. Aegis says the dashed edge is what he meant, since an agent can now bring back something you deleted; he would also accept it solid with the agent's ring if the founder prefers that.

The turn strip is the board's one place for undo, because no key may stand on a board. When an agent's turn changes or removes another hand's marks, or undoes something you did, a notice row takes the you row's place: "Claude in billing-ui changed 3 · removed 2 of your marks · 14:20", with **undo** and **dismiss**. Changed and removed are always counted apart. "Claude undid your change" is always said and is never cut from the row. Every notice stays until you press one of its keys, so the board keeps that change undoable; that includes one agent changing another's marks, since watching agents work together is part of the job. Up to eight stand. Older ones are behind a small `+2 ▾` key, which opens a menu with an undo on each row, so you never have to give up a newer notice to reach an older one. While your pointer is on the strip, nothing in it changes: a new notice waits behind `+k`, and a key takes no press in the first half second after it appears or changes, so a double click can't undo and then redo. Undo, redo and put back always stand in the same column; dismiss and show chat in the column to their right. When the words don't fit beside the keys, as in a narrow leaf, the row becomes two lines. If you select one mark that another hand changed, the same slot shows that mark's own row with one undo key, which undoes only that change to that mark. A mark of yours that was removed shows as four corner ticks, a cut-out, while its notice stands, and you can put it back on its own. Undo works field by field. Anything another hand changed afterwards is left alone, and the line under the head says so: "Undid Claude's turn · 2 marks changed since were left as they are". For six seconds after an undo, the row reads "Undone · …" and the key reads **redo**. The keys are quiet keys in fixed slots. They take only a trusted press of their own, always name the exact change their row showed, and are dropped, never replayed, while anything is pending or the link is down. They never use a decision's words, a letter, ⌘Z or a voice phrase.

With several agents, you can see who is changing what on the marks as each change lands, in the foot (who may edit), and in a notice at the end of each turn. Pointing at a notice outlines that turn's marks on the board in a dashed line, and pointing at a hand in the foot outlines every mark that hand drew, changed or wrote into. Undoing a single mark needs a pointer for now, because marks can't yet be selected from the keyboard; the whole-turn undo on the notice is the keyboard's way. Turning one agent's key off stops that agent and leaves the others working. If its turn was still running, the notice comes straight away, so stopping and undoing takes two presses. A list of every turn past the eight that stand, per-hand filters and stepping the board back wait for the history slice. When an agent's edit is refused because another hand got there first, nothing shows to you: the board is as the other hand left it.

The draw key now reads **may edit** or **may not edit**, because a grant now lets an agent change and remove your marks too. Aegis's wording, "may draw and change marks", does not fit the foot at a 1024 window. The key's title says the whole thing: "Claude may draw on this board and change or remove any mark, yours included · press to stop it". The first session, which may edit from the start without a press, hears that sentence once.

What the founder should look at first: the countermark at the foot and the dashed rule under another hand's words; the dashed edge on your own mark when an agent put it back; "may edit" on the draw key; the undo and dismiss pair in the turn strip, and a notice that stays until you answer it; the cut-out for a removed mark; the strip growing by a row on a short board while a notice stands; the dashed outline when you point at a notice or a hand; and agent-on-agent notices standing until answered.
