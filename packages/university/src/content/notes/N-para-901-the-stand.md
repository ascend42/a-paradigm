## Concurrence Anesthetizes

Ordinary review fails in a specific, quiet way: when everyone nods, scrutiny goes to sleep. If the first thing a gate hears is agreement — "three peers concurred" — the gate certifies by comfort, and comfort is exactly the feeling a wrong-but-plausible learning produces. THE STAND, the gated review at the heart of `/class`, is built to keep the gate awake.

> Concurrence anesthetizes. A gate that opens on agreement is asleep; THE STAND opens on dissent.

## The Turn Order

THE STAND runs in a fixed order, and the order is the point:

1. **The claim — one line.** The proposer states the learning in a single sentence with only thin evidence ("applied 6×, held 6×"). No persuasion, no essay.
2. **The strongest breaking scenario — FIRST.** A *cross-lens* assessor (a different domain than the learner — same-family peers share blind spots) presents the hardest refutation, with Jinx the advocate as standing adversary.
3. **The one causal question.** Asked of the human, with only the dissent and the breaking scenarios on screen.
4. **Interrogation (optional, on the record).** The human may push an `@agent` further.
5. **The verdict.** Refine, promote, or reject.

The Trial GUI reinforces the order: it opens already scrolled to the dissent, with concurrence collapsed behind a muted "+N concurring — show," and the **Refine** button weighted as the primary action.

## No Scenario, No Assessment

An assessor's judgment counts only if it is backed by a concrete breaking scenario. An opinion with no test case is a vibe, not an assessment, and the gate refuses it outright. This rule is what forces genuine cross-lens scrutiny — a builder, a compliance lens, an advocate each bringing a real probe — instead of a show of hands. If nobody can produce a breaking scenario, that is not a pass; it is a reason to withhold (see the last section).

## The One Causal Question

With only the dissent visible, the human is asked the question the whole gate turns on: **does this learning hold *because of its rule*, or were the test cases just easy?** It forks two ways — *rule now* (the rule is sound; certify it) or *interrogate* (push the agent further). A proposer that concedes a limit on the record is not a failure of the gate; it is the loop earning its keep.

## The Verdict: Refine / Promote / Reject

Three outcomes. Promote certifies the learning as written (provisional, pending the field). Reject leaves it staged to decay. And then there is refine — the one most people misunderstand.

> Refine is the engine, not a dial. The counterexample doesn't lower a score — it rewrites the rule as "X except Y."

Refine takes the surviving breaking scenario and folds it back into the learning as an *exception*: the rule is rewritten "X except Y," with the breaking scenario linked as the exception's source. This is not a confidence tweak and not a demotion — it is the mechanism by which the Academy gets *sharper*. A universal claim that broke once becomes a precise claim that holds. Refine-as-localization is why the gate makes the team smarter rather than just gating it.

## Unbroken Is Untested — Never a Silent Pass

Lesson 1's principle returns at the gate. A candidate that *no one managed to break* is **untested, not strong** — and it must never slide through on that silence. The honest move is to promote it as explicitly provisional, or to hold it for a harder scenario, but never to wave it past as if survival-by-absence-of-attack were proof.

## Worked Example: Rune's Double-Tilde Lesson

In the first real term on this project, **Rune** (compliance) took the stand with a learning about aspect validation. **Kit** (builder), a deliberately cross-lens assessor, refuted its breadth with the breaking scenario `SC-aspect-validate-vs-queryable`. The human ruled **REFINE**, and the learning was rewritten: *"validate ≠ aspect_check — EXCEPT bare-key aspects are queryable post-fix."* It was promoted **provisional**, pending field survival — never straight to certified, because only the field certifies. And the repeat-failure-rate stayed `null`: a fresh provisional ruling is not a *settled* exam, so the honest board still showed nothing. That is refine-as-sharpening, end to end.
