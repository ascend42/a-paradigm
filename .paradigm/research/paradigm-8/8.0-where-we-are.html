2 · What changed today, and why

Gnomon design system done

Atelier now keeps one design system per product. We mapped four products (Gnomon, Warpline, Platform, the site), drafted all four, and you parked all but Gnomon. You decided 8 contested questions, and the system lives in .atelier/systems/gnomon/. The UI was then migrated in 7 batches, each reviewed rendered by Mika: copy, field edges and status colour, chips vs keys, toasts, the four-step type scale, spacing and radius, menus and headings.

Why: the UI had 17 font sizes, 8 chip styles and 4 ways to show a notice, so every change re-decided the same things. Now atelier check goes from 1 failure and 174 warnings to 0 failures and 6 warnings, and new UI has a record to follow.

Dictate takes everything said done

Your short phrases were dropped as "speaker not recognised". The voice check refuses anything under 1.5 seconds of speech, and the kernel then refused to forward it. In Dictate, nothing sends without your Enter, so the check was blocking you for no safety gain. Now every turn fills the draft and only Enter sends it. Talk, Send word and Pieces still check the speaker, because they send without a key.

Why it's still safe: Aegis found and we closed a real hole: switching to Send word mid-draft could have sent unverified text. A sent turn containing unverified words also makes Claude ask before edits. Other people's speech is kept out of the permanent log (only a hash is stored) and only your own GUI sees it.

Why Gnomon couldn't be installed by anyone else diagnosed

  • Every Gnomon package was private, so npm i never delivered it.
  • Running it meant cloning the repo and building it with Node 24.
  • The helper (Touch ID, Secure Enclave keys) and the voice binary are Swift apps signed only on your Mac. Anyone else would need Xcode and your signing identity.
  • The app shell had your Node's path and hash compiled in, so it couldn't run on another Mac.

The 8.0 plan you approved decided

you build & sign once per release→npm platform packages→user: npm i -g @a-company/paradigm→gnomon→2 Touch ID prompts→the desk

Decision TD-2026-10-21-758: bundled Node, npm platform packages, signing by a script on your Mac, and voice models from upstream with our mirror as a fallback.

3 · What you need to do, in order

  1. you Test the dev build (section 1) and tell me what's off.
    Why: today's UI and voice changes need your daily use, not just tests.
  2. you M0: signing setup (T-2026-10-21-027). Most of this is already done. I checked your Mac on 22 Oct.
    Why signing is needed at all. Three separate jobs depend on it:
    1. Gatekeeper. macOS refuses to open an app downloaded onto another Mac unless it's signed with a Developer ID and notarized (Apple scans it and issues a ticket that's stapled to the app). Without it, every user sees "Gnomon can't be opened" with no button to proceed.
    2. The Secure Enclave keys. Each user's root and approvals keys live in their Mac's Secure Enclave, inside a keychain access group (SW4BMQ8PNL.gnomon.prod). macOS only lets an app use that group if it's signed by your team and carries a provisioning profile that grants the group. That's what keeps any other app on the user's Mac from using their Gnomon keys.
    3. Gnomon's own trust checks. The kernel and wrapper refuse a helper, Node or voice binary unless it carries your team's signature (and, from M1, the exact hash of the release build). An unsigned build is refused by design.
    Signing happens once per release, on your Mac, with your identity. Users never sign anything; they get your signed app.

    The one real decision: keychain separation

    Apple's Developer ID profile grants your team's whole keychain namespace (SW4BMQ8PNL.*), not just Gnomon's group. So in principle, any other app you sign with your team (a future tool, an experiment) could ask for the gnomon.prod group and use your users' Gnomon keys. Aegis flagged this (helper README, "Keychain separation"). Today the only team-signed binaries are Gnomon's own, so the risk is about the future.

    Why decide before 8.0: users' keys are bound to that access group the moment they first run Gnomon. Changing it after release means every user re-creates their identity.

    What I don't know yet: whether Apple lets a Developer ID profile be narrowed to one group, or whether the only real fix is a rule ("never sign anything else with this team"). I don't want to guess about Apple's portal. Next step: Aegis and I research it and bring you exact portal steps or a written rule, before the signed release build. Nothing for you to click until then.
